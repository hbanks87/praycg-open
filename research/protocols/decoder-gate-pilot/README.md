# Decoder Gate behavioral pilot

This study asks how narrative order and reading instructions relate to understanding and next-day memory. It is an exploratory behavioral pilot. A difference between conditions would not by itself establish a latent “decoder gate.”

The **v3 human-authored literary pilot** uses 32 main passages, two practice passages and four calibration passages. Participants read at their own pace, complete two 16-passage sessions, and return for the matching follow-up about 24 hours after each session. Optional reading feedback is kept separate from correctness.

| Route | Experience | What it can support |
|---|---|---|
| Natural reading | Coherent narrative, no digits | A reading/reference description |
| Comparison | Coherent or reordered text, with narrative or analytic instructions; digits are summed only under analytic instructions | The planned four-condition comparison |

Choose one route before practice and keep it fixed. No passage repeats in a different condition for the same participant. Participants should follow their operator's instructions and avoid previewing source books or researcher materials before follow-up.

## Which version is available?

The [public Workbench 11.12.0 release](https://github.com/hbanks87/praycg-open/releases/tag/PRAYCG_Workbench_Public_A11.12.0) includes the managed **v3** instrument. Keep the two versions’ records, materials and results separate. Managed v3 is behavior-only.

# PRAYCG Master Theory and Protocol

**Public research edition v3 | November 8, 2026 | Hoyt Banks**

## About this edition

PRAYCG asks whether receiving a story produces a measurable physiological and behavioral trajectory that differs from disrupted audiovisual structure and from the same intact material approached as an analytic task. It also asks what continues after the stimulus: recognition, integration, relief, recovery, or a mixture. The experimental distinctions are central to the project.

This master restores the detailed PRAYCG3 research account from public v1.1 while retaining the reconstructed Topo-OSM theory, corrected mathematics and evidence review of v2.4. PRAYCG4 and the Semantic Meaning Gradient remain distinct extensions. Decoder Gate and OptoPING retain their own predictions, assumptions and unresolved tests. The Workbench supports these studies and other research; its sophistication does not determine whether a particular hypothesis is true.

Chapters 1-4 explain the question, theory and experiments. Chapters 5-7 describe acquisition, analysis and recorded findings. Chapters 8-11 cover the newer investigations, research workflow and next tests. Chapter 12 and Appendix F account for every numbered section of v1.1. The technical appendices retain the equations and distinguish an implemented calculation from a proposed interpretation.

The software reference is the packaged Public Alpha 11.11.0 release. Its source inspections, retrospective equipment checks and documented limits are identified separately from the earlier human studies. This document does not add participant data or rerun the archived scientific analyses. Ongoing pilot revisions are not silently adopted. Earlier documents and recordings remain unchanged.

## Evidence labels

**Preserved proposal** identifies the original research idea. **Mathematical correction** repairs a specific algebraic, dimensional or interpretive statement. **New formalization** identifies a declared modeling choice. **Observed result** refers to a saved recording or test within its stated scope. **Empirical background** identifies published work relevant to a component of the proposal. **Implementation finding** describes what a versioned worker actually calculates. None of these labels makes a proposed construct an established measurement.

# 1. Purpose, questions and evidence

PRAYCG investigates how an experience changes a person's state, how something previously learned becomes available again, and how an organized trace can persist while the system that carries it changes. Its original theory connects encoding, cue-sensitive retrieval and maintenance under molecular turnover. Its human research asks how narrative reception, recognition, integration and after-state can be distinguished from attention, sensory drive and ordinary recovery.

Topo-OSM is the organizing theory, with separate cellular and human-scale formulations. Decoder Gate concerns access and receiver state; the meaning extensions concern consequential updating; OptoPING proposes possible reciprocal bridges between a structural state and electrical activity. These ideas constrain proposed tests, but they are not established mechanisms. Chapter 2 restores their relationship, and Appendix A states the repaired mathematics.

The open, local-first PRAYCG Workbench is the research infrastructure for those tests. It organizes protocols, stimulus preparation, hardware, synchronized recording, analysis, explanations and bundles. It is also intended to support independent studies beyond PRAYCG's own hypotheses. Comprehension, recognition, emotional impact, later recall and physiological persistence remain separate outcomes; a study must measure their relationships rather than assume one score represents them all.

## The question behind the protocol

The original master framed the question directly: when a story is allowed to matter, does the body-brain system follow a different trajectory from recognizable structure being disrupted or from the intact story watched under analytic demand? That question remains the starting point [P0]. Its experimental form requires separate observations of what a participant saw, heard, understood, recognized, reported and continued to carry afterward.

A film changes luminance, motion, cuts, speech, music, expectation and emotional context together. Watching it also changes gaze, posture, breathing and muscle activity. PRAYCG was designed to ask which parts of a response survive comparisons with those alternatives. A striking trace during Target is an observation to explain; a pattern across declared contrasts is stronger evidence for a specified prediction.

The original account distinguished six layers:

| Layer | Question to investigate |
| --- | --- |
| Sensory tracking | How much of the response follows measured audiovisual features? |
| Local recognizable structure | Do faces, objects, bodies and intact shots explain the difference? |
| Narrative reception | What does coherent sequence add, and is comprehension actually demonstrated? |
| Analytic task stance | How does instructed cue monitoring and calculation change the response? |
| Integration and after-state | What persists, changes or returns after the material ends? |
| Receiver availability | How do access, prior knowledge, familiarity and present state alter reception? |

These layers are related questions rather than a causal decomposition already established by the controls. They give each comparison a purpose and make it possible to state what remains unresolved.

## Availability and the receiver

The participant brings a learned history to the stimulus. A cue can evoke a rich response without presenting every detail of the memory again; a fully audible story can be recognized without producing a large new update. Relevance, recognition, comprehension, emotional impact, later recall and physiological persistence therefore remain separate outcomes. Their association is part of the research question.

The original availability-under-load expression combined coherence, adaptive capacity and persistence against mismatch and load. Here it remains an organizing proposal, with no universal numerical scale assigned to those terms. The newer Decoder Gate studies provide narrower operational tests. Their results cannot by themselves identify every process named in the broad proposal.

Participant availability and measurement availability require different records. A person may be unable to follow masked dialogue; alternatively, they may understand it while a poorly coupled electrode prevents analysis. Artifact burden can obscure a response without demonstrating a closed biological gate. No latent gate should be inferred merely because a predicted physiological effect was absent. Independently observed access, behavior, task adherence and uncertainty constrain that interpretation.

The experimental sequence is:

```text
assigned material and task
  -> observed behavior and physiology
  -> source, timing and quality checks
  -> comparisons with competing explanations
  -> interpretation and a reproducible study record
```

The Workbench's contribution is continuity across this sequence: protocol, materials, hardware identity, acquisition, analysis inputs and reports should refer to the same study. It records inconsistencies rather than silently guessing a channel, selecting an unrelated output or treating a planned analysis as completed [W1-W4]. This is useful engineering even while particular research measures remain exploratory.

## What the evidence establishes

| Evidence | What it establishes | Remaining question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspected implementation | A specified workflow or calculation exists | Does it behave correctly on other inputs? |
| Synthetic or fixture test | Behavior on declared cases, including failures | Does that behavior generalize to real recordings? |
| Measured functional test | Observed operation of a particular device/configuration | Are calibration, reliability and physical timing adequate for the intended endpoint? |
| Exploratory human recording | A pattern in the recorded conditions | Do order, stimulus, task or artifact explain it? |
| Prospective held-out evaluation | Performance of a frozen prediction in a defined design | Does it generalize to new people, materials or settings? |
| Independent reproduction | Another operator can obtain or reanalyze the result | Could both studies share a measurement or design error? |

An unavailable endpoint is not zero; a failed scientific criterion is different from a software crash. Repeated analyses of one acquisition are not replications. Measures sharing inputs are not independent confirmations.

The current evidence does not establish a consciousness measure, semantic-specific neural gate, optical memory mechanism, thermodynamic weight of a thought or clinical effect. It supports inspectable software, specific exploratory measurements, recorded pilot observations and falsifiable proposals. Chapters 2 and 7-9 distinguish the proposals, findings and tests still required.

PRAYCG's candidate contributions include its enforced study identity, measured-reference gamma comparisons, optical timing, affordable multimodal adaptations and particular combinations of experimental controls. Their value should be tested through error prevention, incremental prediction, independent builds and reproducible measurements. Existing signal-processing, provenance and narrative methods are upstream foundations. Neither integration nor a new name alone establishes scientific priority.

# 2. Topo-OSM: memory, meaning and maintained organization

## 2.1 The theory this project is trying to test

The original question is how an experience can leave an organized trace, how a later cue can reactivate it, and how that organization can persist while the physical system continues to change. PRAYCG brings this question to narrative experience: what happens when a person recognizes, receives, revises or continues to carry a story? Topo-OSM supplies a proposed account of maintained organization and state change. The recording and analysis tools supply ways to challenge parts of that account. The organizing hypothesis is that experience can alter a distributed, retrievable organization whose maintenance and later effects depend on the receiver's prior state and continuing dynamics.

The theory contains two related but distinct hypotheses. **OSM-CELL** proposes a contribution from intracellular structural states to encoding and later neural response. **OSM-TOP** proposes that human-scale activity can reveal changes in state organization, persistence and reactivation. Neither is established by naming an EEG feature after it. A finding at one level does not automatically identify a mechanism at the other [T1, T5].

This edition restores those questions without treating their original equations as settled laws. It preserves the encoding-storage-retrieval sequence, distributed memory, refresh under turnover, receiver history and meaning-related state change. It also identifies where earlier formulations were revised and where mathematical definitions still require operational measurement.

## 2.2 Encoding and cue-sensitive retrieval

The early OSM proposal begins with neural activity, calcium entry and biochemical change. A calcium/CaMKII-linked phosphorylation state is hypothesized to alter a tryptophan-related or microtubule response spectrum. These proposed storage sites are part of the original hypothesis, not established memory substrates. A later drive near that altered response could then produce a stronger response than a detuned drive. The intended memory claim concerns a history-dependent change in responsiveness, not simply activity continuing after a stimulus [T1].

The network patch makes encoding conditional on more than sensory intensity. Incoming drive, inhibition, prediction error and plasticity-related gain may jointly influence whether an episode changes the storage variables. Familiar input could be processed or recognized without producing the same update as an unexpected but comparable input. These are model hypotheses: novelty, surprise and neuromodulatory gain are not interchangeable, and increasing voltage does not universally increase calcium current because receptor opening and driving force both matter [T2].

A scalar storage variable can represent a degree of change, but cannot describe an arbitrary memory's content. The distributed extension therefore proposes a vector or field across sites or compartments. Retrieval depends on overlap between the cue and that learned pattern, with competing patterns considered. This preserves an important distinction: a partial cue can elicit a rich response without carrying all the retrieved information itself.

The oscillator example illustrates conditional selectivity. It does not prove that a biological memory is a tuned oscillator, that its response is detectable at the scalp, or that the largest gamma response is successful recall. The source, coupling, competing responses and behavioral retrieval criterion all need independent evidence.

## 2.3 What persists when the parts are replaced?

The refresh theory addresses molecular turnover directly. A decades-long memory need not contain decades-old proteins; the proposed persistent object is an organized pattern maintained through replacement and reactivation. Its schematic state is [T3, T12]:

```text
X_mem(t) = [P_tr(t), B(t), W(t), Z(t), R(t)]
```

| Variable | Proposed role | What must be specified |
| --- | --- | --- |
| P_tr | Structural trace or pattern | Observable identity, location and history-dependent change |
| B | Stabilizing scaffold state | Which measured processes alter turnover or support |
| W | Synaptic/circuit organization | The task-relevant pattern and its relation to retrieval |
| Z | Transcriptional/regulatory support | Rebuilding conditions, not a literal memory file |
| R | Replay, recall or reactivation | Independently identified activity and its maintenance effect |

The notation X_mem distinguishes the proposed memory architecture from a score of meaning; P_tr distinguishes a structural trace from the phosphorylation fraction used in Appendix A.1. These are notation clarifications, not additional measured states.

Memory maintenance through reactivation and biochemical feedback already has explicit synaptic models; Smolen's model is one relevant comparison [R8]. The distinct question here is whether the proposed structural variables add independently measurable explanatory value. This is an architecture for a hypothesis, not evidence that every memory requires all five components. Trace strength, information content and retrieval success also remain different quantities. A stable scalar amplitude could conceal replacement by a different pattern; preservation therefore requires a declared fidelity measure as well as persistence.

The earlier shorthand said refresh must exceed loss. That captures a useful intuition but is not a universal threshold theorem for the proposed coupled equations. Continuing input can sustain a nonzero trace even below a nominal refresh/loss ratio, while an unstable feedback loop can amplify activity without preserving memory. The corrected task is to examine bounded dynamics, stable states, perturbation recovery and pattern fidelity under the actual assumptions. Appendix A.3 gives the precise threshold for one saturating self-renewal model and a separate error-correction model. A candidate refresh process earns support through measurements of the proposed variables and their contribution to retrieval.

**Empirical background.** In mouse hippocampal engram ensembles, Marco and colleagues observed chromatin changes across encoding, consolidation and reactivation [R12]. Das and colleagues observed repeated Arc transcription and local translation after a single stimulation in a neuronal preparation [R13]. These findings make regulatory support and sustained molecular renewal reasonable processes to investigate. They do not identify the proposed OSM structural trace, establish decades of information preservation, or show that every memory needs the five listed components. Turnover rates must be measured for the actual components rather than assumed to be uniform.

**Hypothesis still requiring a discriminating test.** A specified structural or regulatory variable should improve prediction of trace-specific fidelity or retrieval beyond a credible synaptic and reactivation account that already allows plasticity, turnover and maintenance. Perturbations must verify target engagement, cover the relevant timescale and control for impaired viability, sensation or retrieval. Failure to recall can reflect impaired access; it is not sufficient evidence that the stored pattern was erased.

## 2.4 Candidate bridges to neural activity

The membrane-coupling proposal leaves several physical possibilities open: optical, electrical, dielectric or vibrational/structural modes could, if measurable, influence membrane threshold, gain or timing. It introduces a transfer process between an intracellular state and an E/I network, rather than replacing synaptic dynamics [T4].

These alternatives have different tests. An optical pathway needs a source spectrum, photon supply, receptor or transducer, losses and a downstream effect. An electrical or mechanical pathway needs its own source amplitude, transfer characteristics and intervention controls. Success for one route cannot be assumed for the others.

Optical carrier frequency must remain separate from EEG gamma. A hypothetical slow envelope or beat still needs effective electrical transduction and a source-to-sensor path. The absence of such a demonstrated path cannot be repaired by matching two plotted rhythms. The later photon-budget study tests one candidate bridge; it does not replace the broader structural-memory question.

## 2.5 The explicit human-scale reformulation

Topo-OSM deliberately changed the human-facing terminology. Here **Opto** refers to structured audiovisual input; **Structural** refers to proposed network-state organization or geometry; **Memory** refers to persistence, retrievability and reactivation. This is a reformulation with a different measurement scope, not evidence that the cellular mechanism has been observed [T5].

An inferred state can summarize EEG, autonomic, stimulus and artifact features. Calling that state topological does not itself establish topology. A study must declare whether it measures connectivity, state-space geometry, recurrence or another property, and how that property is distinguished from ordinary amplitude and autocorrelation. Otherwise the result should remain a feature-state proxy.

Likewise, reciprocal prediction between a work signal and a state proxy does not establish a self-maintaining causal loop. Shared feature inputs, common stimulation and measurement noise can create association. Persistence, reactivation and retrieval need their own outcomes; they cannot all be inferred from a coupling score.

| Formulation | Preserved contribution | Current status or explicit change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Early OSM [T1] | Biochemical tuning and cue-sensitive retrieval | Conditional mechanism; scalp EEG is indirect |
| Network patch [T2] | Encoding depends on network state; memory can be distributed | Candidate model; correct the unconditional voltage/current inference |
| Refresh architecture [T3] | Pattern maintenance across turnover | Retained; replace the universal ratio threshold with model-specific analysis |
| Membrane bridge [T4] | Candidate intracellular-to-network coupling | Optical, electrical and structural routes remain distinct |
| Topo-OSM [T5] | Human state organization, persistence and reactivation | Explicitly separated from cellular claims |
| Meaning extensions [T6-T8] | Update geometry, adaptive use and stimulus gradients | Operational proposals needing measurement validation |
| Wild Hare synthesis [T9] | Receiver history connects recognition, update and continuing readiness | Speculative synthesis, not a result or additional physical law |

## 2.6 Meaning depends on the receiver

The meaning-state formulation asks how an experience relates to a person's model of self, others, world or situation. The key-and-decoder metaphor expresses the preserved proposal: the stimulus supplies structured input, while learned history and present conditions shape what it can evoke. An experience may reactivate a familiar interpretation, alter an expectation, resolve an uncertainty or change what actions seem possible [T6, T9].

This account does not require every meaningful experience to cause a large belief update. A familiar scene can still matter deeply when its content is already expected. Recognition and relevance therefore remain measurable dimensions in their own right. A probability divergence is one candidate measure of change within this broader account. It does not replace the account with a single universal unit of meaning.

| Dimension | Candidate observation | Interpretation boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition and reactivation | Independently scored recognition and cue-specific retrieval | Familiarity or a returning EEG pattern alone does not establish retained content |
| Personal relevance | Declared ratings and independently assessed choices or consequences | Relevance does not establish accuracy, benefit or a shared response |
| Belief updating | Before-and-after probabilities about the same specified outcomes | Large change can be mistaken, harmful or unrelated to the intended construct |
| Retention and transfer | Delayed recall or performance on new items | Immediate comprehension does not establish durable integration |
| Interpersonal understanding | Inferences compared with another person's independent reports | Similar responses and physiological synchrony are insufficient on their own |

These observations are reported separately. Combining them would require a new, justified measurement model, with weights and missing-data rules fixed before evaluation. Their relationship is a hypothesis to test.

**Mathematical correction.** D198's posterior-to-prior Kullback-Leibler divergence is retained as model-specific updating, U_i, rather than being called meaning itself. Its relationship to Bayesian surprise has established precedent [R9]. Probabilities must describe the same state space and be collected or estimated by a declared procedure. An arbitrary vector of impact ratings cannot be inserted into the divergence as if it were a probability distribution. Appendix A.7 specifies support, units and estimation limits.

**Preserved interpersonal proposal with a narrower name.** D198's metric cosine becomes update alignment. It measures whether two reliable change vectors point in similar directions in a shared, fixed coordinate system. Two people accepting the same false claim can align strongly. A person who understands another's distress may respond with calm concern rather than the same emotional change. Empathic accuracy therefore requires a separate comparison between an observer's inference and the target's own independently collected report, with report error acknowledged [R10]. Concern, affect sharing and helping behavior also need their own observations. The alignment score is not an empathy scale until that interpretation survives such tests.

**Predictive validity remains separate.** Before-and-after forecasts should be scored against the same later outcomes that were unavailable when the forecasts were made. An improvement can support a limited prediction claim. Feeling certain or internally coherent does not establish that improvement. Recall of a fictional story measures accuracy about that story, not truth about the external world. High reported significance alongside a false belief would be compatible with the proposed separation; one such result would not validate the whole theory.

Communication in these studies uses ordinary sensory and social channels. Relational geometry names a declared pattern of relationships or state coordinates. It does not establish quantum entanglement, a universal entropy law favoring social connection, or a physical quantity of meaning in rocks or stars. Those philosophical extensions do not follow from D198 or from the PRAYCG measurements. The human research remains centered on individual reception, recognition, update and after-state; cross-person alignment is an optional additional question.

## 2.7 Recognition, integration, peaks and resolution

Recognition and durable update need not coincide. A familiar scene might activate an existing pattern without adding much new information. Conversely, understanding something at first exposure need not produce lasting retention. The research question is whether separately measured comprehension, later retrieval, prediction or transfer distinguish these possibilities. A missing theta endpoint cannot establish that no learning occurred.

The peak/resolution distinction preserves another part of the original intent. One response might involve abrupt accommodation to an unexpected interpretation; another might involve the resolution of a previously active uncertainty. Their immediate intensity, regulatory demands and later trajectories could differ. These remain competing descriptions to test, not types already established by the historical recordings [T9].

For either account, persistence must be distinguished from fatigue, ordinary recovery, continued attention, rehearsal and artifact. Re-presenting an appropriate cue could test reactivation; independently scored retention or transfer could test consequences. Washout is useful observation time, but a slow physiological return alone does not identify what was retained.

## 2.8 Meaning gradients and constructive updating

The semantic-gradient proposal separates coherent low-human-stakes material from chaotic randomness and from the phase-scrambled sensory control. It asks whether independently assessed stimulus properties and participant responses predict different trajectories. The proposed density and carryover indices remain declared measurements, not objective units of meaning. Stimulus coding should be independent of the physiological endpoint. Manipulation checks must not discard a run merely because its predicted effect failed; that would select evidence using the outcome being tested. Low meaning, high meaning and effective arithmetic override require evidence rather than labels [T8].

Constructive meaning adds a different question: does an interpretation help someone generate usable options, respond to feedback and revise a failing policy? Feeling better, producing more options and making better predictions are separable. D203's intended test includes costs, validity and flexibility, rather than defining every emotionally positive response as adaptive [T7]. The appendix offers one explicitly versioned alternative denominator while preserving signed benefits and costs separately.

## 2.9 Predictions at the appropriate scale

| Hypothesis | Discriminating observation | What would weaken the specified account |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cellular tuning affects retrieval-like response | Measured state change predicts selective response to matched versus detuned drive | No reproducible tuning or specificity after preparation controls |
| A structural mode affects excitability | Independent perturbation changes the proposed mode and membrane response | Effects explained by heating, toxicity, common drive or measurement pickup |
| Refresh preserves a trace through turnover | Pattern fidelity and retrieval track specified maintenance processes | Prespecified disruptions leave the predicted contribution unsupported |
| Human state organization carries information beyond current input | Frozen state features improve held-out prediction of reactivation or behavior | Simple stimulus/history models perform equally well |
| Recognition and new integration can dissociate | Separate recognition and later-update outcomes vary as predicted | The proposed distinction adds no reliable predictive value |
| Meaning-frame benefits depend on updating | Improved usable options and feedback adaptation beyond affect/task alternatives | Positivity increases without the predicted behavioral benefit |

Cellular tests require appropriate preparations and direct measurements. Human narrative studies can test behavioral and physiological predictions without assuming a molecular bridge. Neither scale should borrow the other's evidence to fill a missing step. The restored theory is a connected research program about maintained organization and consequential experience, with each bridge accountable to its own test.

**Additional rejection criteria for this reconciliation.** A study must choose which claim it is testing. A null result for an EEG proxy does not refute every possible account of meaning or memory. Conversely, a broad theory cannot be protected by switching its endpoint after a failed prediction.

- An update-only definition of meaning is inadequate if independently reliable relevance or recognition remains high while the specified probabilities do not change. This challenges that definition, not the possibility of familiar meaningful experience.
- An empathy interpretation of update alignment is weakened if it adds no held-out prediction of independently measured empathic accuracy beyond shared stimulus, response style and task. Wide uncertainty remains inconclusive.
- A truth-tracking prediction is weakened if posterior forecasts fail to improve the prespecified proper score on the same held-out outcomes, despite adequate measurement precision. The size of the update cannot substitute for this test.
- A specified structural refresh model is weakened if its measured variables add no predictive value over credible maintenance alternatives, or if a selective, adequately long perturbation leaves its predicted fidelity effect absent. Unmeasured variables or an ineffective intervention leave that test unresolved.

# 3. PRAYCG3: from baseline to after-state

## 3.1 The experiment's central question

PRAYCG3 asks whether an intact story watched naturally produces a different trajectory from a version with recognizable narrative structure damaged, and from the same intact material watched while performing an analytic task. The object of study is the trajectory: access to the material, recognition during viewing, subsequent processing, and the state carried into the periods after viewing. No single increase in EEG activity establishes that this sequence occurred [P0].

The original three-branch design remains the clearest introduction to that question. PhaseScrambled Control challenges a sensory-drive explanation. Target presents the intact story under natural viewing instructions. Contextual Override preserves that material while changing what the participant is asked to do. Baselines, washouts and reports make the transitions observable. Their roles differ, and their observations must remain identifiable in the study record.

## 3.2 The complete sequence

The legacy PRAYCG3 order is fixed: Control, Target, Override. The following sequence restores its full structure, including a consequential detail of the current implementation: the Override sum response precedes its washout, while the branch experience report follows the washout [P0, W5].

| Stage | Participant activity | Research purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Preparation | Confirm display/audio access, instructions, recording readiness and assigned material | Establish what can be presented and measured |
| Baseline 1 | Remain quietly at fixation | Describe the initial state and provide eligible calibration support |
| PhaseScrambled Control | Watch naturally | Observe response to transformed sensory material |
| Washout 1, then report | Remain quietly, then describe the experience | Separate post-Control physiology from report activity |
| Target | Watch the intact cued story naturally; ignore the digits | Observe reception under natural viewing instructions |
| Washout 2, then report | Remain quietly, then describe the experience | Observe the immediate post-Target trajectory |
| Contextual Override | Watch the same cued story while adding the digits | Observe the effect of assigned analytic work |
| Sum response and stillness | Enter the final sum, then settle | Record task performance and isolate response activity |
| Washout 3, then report | Remain quietly, then describe the experience and task | Observe the post-task trajectory with its intervening response documented |
| Baseline 2 / final reflection | Quietly reflect on and compare all completed branches | Observe the cumulative state under a declared reflection instruction |
| Final report | Give comparative ratings and relevant context | Preserve the participant's retrospective account |

Instructions and a stillness reset precede each video; a further reset precedes final reflection. The inspected runner defaults to 120 seconds for Baseline 1, each washout and final reflection, with an 8-second stillness reset. These are configurable software defaults, not established optimal durations. The locked study configuration and actual events determine what occurred. Final reflection can be disabled; its absence must remain an absence, not a reconstructed measurement [W5].

An initial baseline is a reference observation, not a state without thoughts, expectations or autonomic variation. Baseline quality, duration and usable coverage determine whether a subsequent normalization is supported. The recording must retain instructions, video boundaries, responses, washouts, reports and deviations as distinct events. Planned timing alone does not establish physical presentation timing.

## 3.3 What the three branches can distinguish

PhaseScrambled Control is a meaning-damaged sensory control. It may preserve selected low-level properties while disrupting recognizable audiovisual organization. It can still provoke entrainment, scanning, boredom, discomfort, confusion, affect or attempts to discover a pattern. Its physiological activity is part of the comparison. Calling it meaningless would mistake an intended manipulation for an observed participant state [P0].

Target is the intact stimulus watched naturally. The instruction permits reception without requiring an emotional response. A participant may follow the story, recognize familiar content, disengage, or experience little personal relevance. These possibilities belong in the data. Target activity becomes informative through comparisons, behavioral evidence and examination of plausible sensory and artifact explanations.

For the instruction-only Target/Override contrast, both branches use the same cue-embedded audiovisual file. The number overlays, their encoded positions and timing therefore match; Target instructs the participant to ignore them, while Override requests a running sum. The current manifest declares Target and Override a bit-identical media group. Different filenames do not establish equivalence, and an uncued Target compared with a cued Override would change the intended contrast [P0, W5].

Override tests task stance. It changes cue monitoring, working memory, arithmetic, effort, visual attention and possibly gaze or muscular tension. It may reduce narrative absorption, leave comprehension intact, or help a participant notice material missed earlier. Meaning may also break through the task. A Target/Override difference consequently supports an effect of the assigned task under the recorded conditions; identifying its psychological pathway requires additional measurements. Correct arithmetic alone does not prove that the story was excluded from processing.

## 3.4 Washouts and the final reflection state

Washout preserves observation time after a branch. It does not guarantee erasure of the preceding experience or restoration of Baseline 1. A story can remain active through imagery, rehearsal, emotion or reflection. Breathing can settle, task effort can subside, and physiology can recover on a different timescale from subjective experience. Those alternatives motivate measurement of both the immediate trajectory and its supporting context [P0].

Reports follow the washout so ordinary rating and typing activity is separated from that observation interval. Override has an additional asymmetry: sum entry and a stillness reset occur between video offset and washout. Post-branch comparisons must preserve elapsed time from video offset and account for that intervening task. Equal washout labels or durations do not make their beginnings equivalent. Response and report periods should not be silently included in passive after-state endpoints.

Baseline 2 is particularly informative and particularly easy to overinterpret. The current instruction asks for quiet reflection on all three blocks. It is therefore a cumulative reflection condition, not an untouched resting baseline or a Target-only follow-up. It can contain narrative carryover, task-completion relief, fatigue, habituation, respiratory change, deliberate comparison and memory reactivation. Its difference from Baseline 1 also includes a difference in instruction [P0, W5].

Attribution should examine earlier washouts, task performance, participant reports and autonomic trajectories together. Resemblance to post-Target activity can motivate a hypothesis, but does not identify its cause. A strong delayed effect with a weak in-clip peak can be compatible with later processing or ordinary recovery; the distinction needs an outcome and comparison capable of separating them.

## 3.5 First-person reports as a separate evidence stream

Self-report preserves what the experience was like for the participant. It can agree with physiology, diverge from it, reveal sensory problems, or indicate a strategy the experimenter did not anticipate. Reports are separate measurements, not automatically statistically independent evidence: the same stimulus, participant history, task and expectations can affect both report and physiology [P0].

The six core branch domains are personal meaning, absorption, emotional afterglow, story activity during washout, task extraction load and confound burden. Supporting context includes audio comprehension, audiovisual synchrony, cue legibility, eye strain or squinting, external noise, familiarity and relevant prior exposure. Override additionally needs task performance and the participant's account of effort and story breakthrough. Final comparisons preserve which branches were experienced as more meaningful, demanding or persistent. The study should distinguish a question that was not administered from a low response [P0, W5].

Scene descriptions should identify a line, image, sound or felt moment without demanding an estimated clock time. Retrospective timing guesses cannot supply precise prospective anchors. Questions should also allow no meaningful moment, no afterglow or an unsuccessful manipulation. Excluding participants because their meaning ratings failed to follow the predicted ordering would select evidence using the outcome the study seeks to explain.

Reports remain fallible, influenced by recall, wording and the demand to explain. Their value is increased by preserving prompts, response timing and missingness, and by keeping comprehension, personal relevance, arousal and task effort distinguishable. A disagreement between report and physiology is a result to investigate; neither stream automatically overrules the other.

## 3.6 Recognition, integration, peaks and resolution

The original account distinguished recognition from integration and an acute peak from a later resolution trajectory. These are useful research axes, not validated participant types. Recognition concerns identifying or reactivating something already available. Integration concerns a proposed change in organization or consequence that must be specified through outcomes such as later retention, retrieval, prediction or transfer. Familiarity can strengthen recognition while reducing novelty; it does not make an experience personally insignificant [P0].

The peak/resolution axis concerns temporal form. An abrupt recognition or reinterpretation may produce a brief candidate event. Closure, repair or resolution may develop more gradually or continue after the clip. Immediate intensity, subsequent calm and retained information need not covary. A slow physiological return does not reveal what was learned, and an unavailable theta endpoint does not establish that learning failed.

The pilot-derived descriptions remain hypotheses: recognition without a clear after-state, an acute event followed by later density, modest in-clip impact with stronger subsequent activity, or a delayed response despite compromised sensory access. The last possibility motivates a decoder-availability question: did partial input reactivate prior knowledge? Each pattern also admits sensory, task, respiratory and artifact alternatives [P0].

CAI/SID can provide a secondary description of declared access-like and integration-like features over supported time [M1]. A peak score, an interval density and a participant report answer different questions. Shared inputs prevent their agreement from counting as independent replications. The inference becomes stronger when a frozen physiological prediction explains a separately measured consequence on held-out data, beyond credible stimulus and participant-history comparisons.

# 4. Controls, extensions and stimulus preparation

## 4.1 PRAYCG4 and the structural-control question

PRAYCG4 extends the three-branch sequence by adding ShotOrderScramble, with its own washout and report. In the legacy order it follows PhaseScrambled Control and precedes Target. The purpose is specific: phase scrambling damages local recognizable structure as well as narrative continuity. A Target/PhaseScrambled difference can therefore reflect faces, bodies, objects or biological motion without requiring a narrative-order explanation [P0].

ShotOrderScramble retains intact local shots while changing their larger order. Depending on the declared audio treatment, it may also preserve local speech fragments. It supplies a middle comparison with more recognizable content than phase scrambling. Reordering nevertheless changes continuity, transitions, expectancy, confusion and sometimes cut rhythm. Participants may reconstruct a familiar plot or find a new interpretation. The manipulation does not isolate meaning perfectly.

A prospective hypothesis may predict a specified Target endpoint exceeding PhaseScrambled, ShotOrderScrambled and Override at locked windows. That direction must belong to the endpoint and theory being tested; every physiological measure need not increase with reception. Report each contrast, uncertainty and nuisance sensitivity. The pattern across controls is more informative than choosing whichever comparison happens to favor the theory.

## 4.2 Semantic Meaning Gradient

The Semantic Meaning Gradient is a separate family. Its original sequence compares coherent low-intended-meaning material, a high-meaning target and arithmetic override. The low-meaning condition should not simply be random noise: randomness can create novelty, threat, puzzle demand or prediction error. Coherence and personal meaning are distinct stimulus and participant properties [P0].

Candidate stimulus coding includes social presence, agency, vulnerability, causal continuity, care or repair, stakes and transformation, with overload and confusion considered separately. These dimensions require declared coding rules and independent characterization. A category name, weighted index or threshold does not create objective units of meaning. Measures of comprehension and participant relevance help establish whether the intended manipulation occurred without defining success by the physiological result.

This family asks whether characterized content and receiver context predict different trajectories. It does not replace the sensory and structural comparisons of PRAYCG3/4. Selecting an accessible story, disrupting its order and changing the participant's task are different experimental interventions.

## 4.3 Order, repetition and familiarity

The fixed PRAYCG3 sequence preserves a historical feasibility design but confounds branch with position. Target normally precedes the intact story's repetition under Override. Familiarity, prediction, learning, fatigue, boredom and carryover can therefore contribute to their difference. Longer washouts do not necessarily remove those influences [P0].

Prospective sequence variants support counterbalanced assignment. Use the actual declared sequence and allocation rule, not the protocol family name, to document balancing. Williams-style or other balanced orders address specified positional and first-order carryover effects when implemented with suitable allocation; they do not eliminate persistent learning, nonlinear carryover or cumulative fatigue [W2, W5].

Record prior viewing, knowledge of the scene, exposure to related material, repeat sessions and relevant context. Novel material can test first-pass reception; familiar material can test reactivation. The same clip can serve as a new story for one person and a cue to an established memory for another. Changing a participant identifier does not erase that history.

Separate development, calibration and evaluation. Hold out whole participants, sessions or stimulus families according to the intended generalization. Overlapping windows from one recording are not independent replications, and random window splitting can transfer nearly identical information into training and evaluation. A study should declare its unit of inference before interpreting a large number of windows as a large sample.

## 4.4 Anchors, null windows and prospective claims

Anchors specify where a candidate recognition, integration or resolution event is expected. Primary peak anchors, secondary context anchors, resolution anchors and clip-edge anchors serve different purposes. Define each window and endpoint before acquisition or before an explicitly independent evaluation. Frame-verify times on the final rendered Target, freeze the anchor file and record its identity with the study [P0].

An anchor chosen from the story is a timing hypothesis. An anchor chosen after inspecting the same physiology is exploratory. Participant descriptions can contextualize either, but must not retroactively convert the latter into a prospective test. For an event near the clip edge, specify whether the endpoint can extend into the washout and whether its analysis method permits crossing that boundary. A continuation hypothesis cannot override a method's requirement for support within one continuous block [M1].

Null or pseudo-anchor windows test how often comparable candidates appear elsewhere. Their selection should respect window duration, usable coverage, temporal dependence and relevant sensory structure. An easy null can exaggerate apparent specificity. Anchor counts, endpoint multiplicity and selection rules require a declared statistical treatment. A successful locked-window result still needs controls and held-out evaluation; freezing a file prevents drift but does not validate its interpretation.

## 4.5 Preparing the stimulus family

Select material with documented source identity, permitted use, content considerations and enough context for the intended audience. The original practical range of roughly 2.5-5 minutes was a stimulus-selection guide, not a validated universal optimum. Clip length should support the intended event, after-state observation and participant burden. Public software distribution does not grant redistribution rights to film excerpts or participant recordings [P0, W2].

Begin from a retained master. Apply any justified cleanup consistently before generating the branches, and document subtitles, source stamps or other overlays. Generate the cue-embedded intact Target and use that same file for Override. Generate the phase-scrambled control from the intended cued source so the transformation includes the cue contribution. Normally generate ShotOrderScramble from the cleaned master and then apply the same cue schedule, retaining the permutation seed and shot table [P0].

The stimulus package should bind the master and derived files to the cue schedule, transformation settings, preparation manifest, visual/audio review, locked anchors and fingerprint outputs. Preserve original materials alongside derivatives. A failed or changed export is a new artifact whose timing and identity must be checked again.

## 4.6 Quality review and fingerprints

Manual viewing should verify playability, duration, frame cadence, tail preservation, cue legibility, overlay treatment and branch identity. Target and Override must meet the declared identity requirement. ShotOrderScramble needs inspection of its actual reordered material, not only a successfully generated permutation. Review the resulting experience for unusually long discontinuities, unintended freezes or other changes that could dominate the comparison [P0, W2].

Control audio requires human listening. Review for recognizable words, sentence fragments or distinctive melodic material that could identify the source, alongside the intended loudness and envelope relation. Automated processing cannot certify unintelligibility. Record who reviewed what version and any residual cues. A failure of the intended manipulation must remain visible rather than being repaired by relabeling the branch.

Fingerprints describe measured audiovisual properties such as luminance, motion, cuts and audio features. Bind each extraction to the exact file, transformation, extraction version, units, time grid and missingness. Shot reordering requires its own temporal mapping; the intact film's timestamps cannot simply be reused [W2].

Fingerprint adjustment asks how much a physiological association changes after accounting for specified measured stimulus features. It does not remove all sensory explanations. Show unadjusted and adjusted results, assess model adequacy, and acknowledge unmeasured features. A weak nuisance model cannot establish that the nuisance is absent. A file hash establishes identity; it does not establish successful playback, physical timing or semantic equivalence.

## 4.7 From a prepared protocol to an interpretable study

Lock the protocol identity, version, ordered branches, instructions, configured durations, media, cue schedule and anchors before recording. Preserve runner identity and actual events. Acquisition should connect these records to the EEG, available autonomic and respiration streams, markers and physical timing evidence. The light sensor measures display behavior; it does not measure a biological response. Suitable eye, muscle or motion measurements can test artifact explanations that scalp EEG alone leaves unresolved [P0, W2].

The original research architecture joined preparation, acquisition, analysis and review so they refer to the same experiment. That continuity remains essential. A completed run, a valid timing observation and an available analysis endpoint are different achievements. Report missing support and failed quality criteria before interpreting trajectories.

The next prospective test should choose its question, contrasts, anchors, outcomes and exclusion rules in advance; obtain adequate sensory, timing and artifact evidence; preserve self-report and task performance; and evaluate the frozen claim on new observations. Recognition, task-dependent reception and persistent consequences can then be tested in their own terms. The protocol earns support by distinguishing these possibilities, including when a simpler explanation accounts for a promising pattern [P0].

## 5.3 Timing is a measurement

Media time, runner callbacks, LSL timestamps, transport arrival and screen luminance describe different events. ALS/PT19 records a physical light response in its own acquisition path, providing delivery evidence beyond software callbacks. It does not establish EEG contact or biological meaning.

The passive dual-ALS comparison fits offset/drift using explicitly matched calibration-event IDs, then evaluates separate held-out pairs. Participant, source, acquisition and segment identities, missing events, gaps and uncertainty remain attached to the result. A common clock label establishes neither common participant identity nor physical sensor synchronization. Alignment must not be optimized to maximize a neural contrast.

Gamma Scalpel's separate dual-ALS helper fits reviewed pulse pairs and builds an engineering envelope from residuals and sample brackets. That envelope is neither a statistical confidence interval nor held-out validation. The full participant-connected qualification of the compact Cerelog/Cyton-Daisy arrangement remains outstanding [H1, H2].

**Retrospective equipment update from Public Alpha 11.11.0.** A corrected detector initialization was applied to the saved six-minute off-body dual-ALS recording. It matched 22 barcode events, with 11 assigned to fitting and 11 to held-out evaluation. The held-out 95th-percentile relative timing error was 7.3006 ms. All nine declared threshold combinations met the original limits; the largest error across that sensitivity review was 18.5398 ms. The historical review and original recording were preserved [H3].

The detector repair and evaluation were retrospective. The held-out event split does not make the overall development process prospective or establish new participant-connected performance. A new full-duration equipment recording and combined body pilot remain necessary. The same release reopens expected-versus-recorded inventories, reporting missing streams, identity/units issues and timestamp anomalies separately from physiological suitability. A bench XDF without the required runner evidence remains an equipment record rather than a completed protocol study [W5, H3].

Device-specific timing limits also matter. Athena's receipt covers connection, EEG identity/units, advancing timestamps, monitor/replay, XDF reopening and disconnection. Its **16 raw optical channels are not 16 anatomical sites**; no verified Athena HbO/HbR conversion profile is bundled. Transport evidence does not establish electrode coupling or physiological accuracy.

Vernier defaults to **100 ms/10 Hz**; **50 ms/20 Hz** requires matching configuration and metadata. The bridge publishes aligned SDK-buffered measurements using approximate host-batch backfilled timestamps. Ambiguous overlapping batches are not silently repaired. Raw force remains distinct from the vendor's derived respiration rate.

Polar RR durations are measured intervals, but intervals arriving together in one BLE notification share its arrival timestamp. Received-order summaries can remain useful while precise event-locked or cardiac-respiratory endpoints require stronger timing evidence. Successive interval differences must preserve original beat adjacency and must not cross reconnect or malformed-packet boundaries [H2].

## 5.4 Preserve the study and its observations

Before launch, bind the session identity to its protocol, permitted baseline/washout settings, prepared material and selected hardware. Changes after locking need a recorded revision. A shortened demonstration must retain its actual duration and purpose. Bench mode supports software demonstrations; exploratory Athena recording remains a legitimate acquisition mode, even when a full-cap endpoint is unavailable.

Keep the original XDF, events, channel/source map, anchors, media manifest and available transport evidence. Record cancellations and incomplete acquisitions honestly. If LabRecorder continues after the protocol ends, preserve the original and select a reviewed analysis interval.

Choose prospective anchors independently of the physiological candidate. Record who chose each anchor, the material/version, frame/time and whether selection preceded acquisition. Pseudo/random anchors can test how often a candidate occurs elsewhere. HED descriptions preserve original events; syntax validation does not establish physical occurrence or timing. Response-bearing files require privacy review [W2].

Behavioral observations need equally clear identities. Preserve question wording, timing, familiarity, effort, absorption and other ratings separately. Comprehension, recognition and delayed recall assess different outcomes; immediate testing can influence later recall. A rating reused inside a composite cannot independently validate it, and an unavailable EEG endpoint does not erase valid behavioral evidence.

## 5.5 The shielded enclosure

Eyes-open/closed material already exists from an enclosure described by the operator as shielded, but its attenuation was not independently characterized in the inspected contract. The planned basement/enclosure PRAYCG4 comparison should measure interference and other artifacts under documented conditions. Keep calibration and tuning separate from the recordings used to judge preservation. The enclosure is a measurement opportunity, not clean ground truth.

# 6. Analysis and signal review

## 6.1 From a recording to an analysis plan

Analysis Forge begins with the completed recording and its associated evidence. Review the run, resolve ambiguous XDFs or sidecars, run required integrity, event/timing, EEG-quality and line-noise checks, then build the supported plan. Behavioral-only Decoder Gate studies use their dedicated report route without an invented XDF [W1-W3].

A contaminated recording can still support descriptive findings. Missing electrodes, events or temporal support can make an endpoint unavailable without invalidating the whole recording. Plans may include downstream modules before prerequisite outputs exist, provided those prerequisites are included and run first. Later changes, recipes and selected processing inputs retain explicit versions.

Results distinguish completed, partial, unavailable, cancelled and software-error outcomes. A report or AI failure remains separate from a numerical failure. Processing history, execution attempts and evidence links allow the reader to establish what actually ran.

The restored rationale follows the original master [P0], compared with the retained Master Suite definitions and current release [M4, W5]. Historical and current method names are distinguished below.

The original method is a sequence of questions about an experience. Did the participant have access to the material? Did physiology track sensory structure, recognition, analytic work or artifact? Did an event coincide with a later change, and did that change persist? The analysis families preserve these questions at different timescales. Their names identify proposed interpretations of measurements; they do not make those interpretations established biological facts.

The logical chain begins with study identity, delivered stimulus, timing, channel identity and usable signal. It then compares candidate responses with sensory, structural, task, respiratory and order explanations. Event-level and continuous summaries follow those checks. Self-report, comprehension and later behavior constrain interpretation. A larger collection of favorable composites cannot compensate for missing timing or establish recognition, encoding or narrative reception without independent outcomes.

## Primary questions, supporting explanations and exploratory candidates

The original master placed timing/QC, stimulus fingerprinting, artifact checks, A-MRED, MRED-Peak and MRED-Resolution in a proposed primary/core path. It placed access, confound and autonomic interpretation in a secondary layer, and KHT and Micro Handoff among exploratory candidates. These were intended study roles, not certificates of validation. Retained software registries also differ: HOC-R, OSA and OHC appear in the original secondary layer but in a later registry's exploratory layer. A study therefore needs its own frozen endpoint hierarchy, worker versions and eligibility rules.

The historical phrase “Target wins all” has only an endpoint-specific meaning. A strict A-MRED comparison asks whether particular Target recognition-like and delayed integration-like measures exceed their declared Control and Override comparators. It does not require Target to maximize every EEG, autonomic or subjective variable. Override may increase task activity or preserve meaningful reception; Control may produce strong sensory responses; resolution may accompany decreasing activation. The direction of each prediction follows its scientific question.

## ArtifactScore, GammaScalpel and the Temporal Semantic Proxy

ArtifactScore exists because eye movements, jaw tension, muscle, movement, electrical interference and unstable voltages can produce an apparent event. Its role is to restrict or qualify interpretation. Passing a composite artifact screen does not establish cortical origin, and unavailable artifact evidence must remain visible. Rejected windows and the duration retained after screening belong beside any resulting contrast.

The legacy GammaScalpel family decomposed lower-gamma activity and combined regional power, phase-locking and artifact sentinels into exploratory work-signal contrasts. Names such as MeaningGamma were operational labels, not measurements of meaning. The current Gamma Scalpel 2.0 and Measured Reference Companion add a distinct comparison of measured EEG with qualifying EMG/EOG evidence. These implementations must not be collapsed into one interchangeable score. Section 6.3 specifies the current screening method; an older result retains its original worker and calibration.

TSP means Temporal Semantic Proxy. Its original purpose was to locate time-resolved recognition/access-like changes that could be compared with narrative events. Retained implementations construct regional or feature contrasts. Electrode groups and residualized combinations do not identify a localized semantic transfer or isolate a semantic generator. TSP becomes informative through its relation to predeclared material, behavior and competing sensory explanations. Gamma and TSP often share inputs, so agreement between them is not independent confirmation.

## MRED and A-MRED: recognition and later change

MRED means Meaning Recognition / Encoding Dissociation. It preserves the hypothesis that recognizing a familiar event can differ from acquiring or integrating something new. Its recognition-like component draws on gamma/TSP and related event measures; its encoding-like component uses later theta/carryover measures and, in retained versions, familiarity or novelty covariates. The labels describe a proposed distinction. Theta is not a direct measurement of successful memory encoding, and a familiarity rating does not independently validate a score that already incorporates it.

Four combinations remain useful hypotheses: strong recognition-like with strong later integration-like evidence; strong recognition-like with weak later evidence; weak recognition-like with strong later evidence; and weak evidence on both axes. A fifth outcome is unavailable or confounded. The third combination calls for checks of task, respiration, novelty, recovery and nonsemantic learning. The second can motivate a recognition or familiar-schema hypothesis, but does not demonstrate that no memory was formed. Later recall, recognition accuracy and transfer are separate measurements needed to test these interpretations.

A-MRED is the anchor-locked version. It asks a narrower question at a scene event whose identity, timing, windows and comparison rules were declared before acquisition. In its historical strict form, Target must satisfy recognition-like and delayed integration-like thresholds and exceed Control and Override by the specified margins after applicable quality and confound checks. Frame verification, content-to-recording alignment and anchor provenance are part of the evidence. A scene explanation supplied after inspecting physiology remains exploratory in that recording.

The delayed window is as consequential as the acute window. Choosing the most favorable delay after inspection changes the question. A window extending beyond a clip requires an explicitly declared continuation policy and adequate support; it cannot silently borrow another branch. A-MRED failure means that the operational endpoint did not pass. It does not establish that the scene was meaningless. A missing required component means the endpoint is ungradable, which differs from an observed negative result.

## MRED-Peak and MRED-Resolution: impact and closure

MRED-Peak and MRED-Resolution were introduced because a single event shape cannot represent every proposed consequence of narrative experience. Peak emphasizes an acute, anchor-linked recognition-like response with delayed integration-like support. Resolution emphasizes a later reflective or regulatory pattern. Retained endpoint-compression software reads other analysis outputs and assembles interpretation tables; it does not supply an independent measurement of either construct.

The distinction allows a modest acute response to coexist with a later candidate for closure or recovery. It also prevents a strong acute response from being described automatically as sustained integration. Neither pattern should be selected retrospectively simply because it favors the hypothesis. Their windows, contrasts and independent behavioral predictions need prospective specification. A final reflection baseline is cumulative after-state: it may include prior branches, task relief, fatigue, respiratory settling and ordinary recovery. Its attribution is a separate analysis problem.

## Confound defenses: what could produce the same pattern?

HOC-R, High-Order Control Residualization, asks whether faces, bodies, local shot content, biological motion or related structure explain an apparent narrative response. ShotOrderScramble strengthens this question by retaining more local structure than PhaseScrambling while altering sequence. Its preservation and disruption must be checked; neither control guarantees the absence of meaning.

OSA, Override Spatial Attention, asks whether viewers sampled an identical Target/Override film differently because they monitored cues, shifted gaze or squinted. File identity controls the media, not the participant's visual behavior. OHC, Order/Habituation/Carryover, addresses position, repetition, fatigue and preceding branches. A washout provides observations of recovery; its name does not prove that the participant reset.

AAM, Afterglow Attribution Model, asks which preceding state best accounts for a later baseline or washout. Target carryover competes with Override task-completion relief and mixed carryover. RespDualPath preserves respiration in two roles: physiological response and potential contributor to EEG or HRV findings. A sigh can be relevant physiology while also reducing confidence in a coincident electrical event. Adjusted and unadjusted analyses answer different questions and should retain their declared purposes.

CET-R, Cinematic Entrainment residualization, tests modeled contributions from luminance, audio envelope, motion, cuts and cue timing. Actual aligned stimulus regressors must be distinguished from a cue-only model or a file inventory. Residual variation is unexplained by the fitted model, not thereby identified as meaning. Missing regressors, model misspecification and fitted-on-the-same-data performance limit the interpretation. These defenses are complementary: residualization cannot replace physical timing, structural controls or measured artifact references.

## NIP and EET: continuous patterns and later resemblance

NIP, Narrative Immersion Proxy, was intended to retain sustained candidate patterns that a single peak can miss. Its retained worker combines access-like and integration-like features with penalties. BIT, CII and IAQ provide threshold, continuous-density and attenuation views of this family. They reuse related measurements, so their joint appearance should be reported as several views of shared evidence. A high composite does not establish subjective immersion, and an attenuation ratio requires an interpretable, sufficiently supported denominator.

EET, Endogenous Echo Tracking, compares selected Target-event feature patterns with later washout or reflection patterns. Resemblance can motivate a persistence or reactivation hypothesis. It cannot identify what information persisted or show that the later pattern was caused by the Target event. Similarity depends on feature selection, normalization and available observations; ordinary recovery or a shared artifact can also resemble an earlier pattern. EET therefore remains exploratory alongside null anchors and competing after-state references.

## CAI/SID and Continuous Autonomic Arrays

CAI is the Controlled Access-Integration Index; SID is State Integration Density. The intended bridge is from isolated events to a supported trajectory: access-like activity, slower integration-like activity, positive-support terms and penalties are combined under a frozen specification. SID summarizes the accepted time represented by that trajectory. Their boundedness is a mathematical property, not a probability of awareness, meaning or memory formation.

The precise current specification and supported-time accounting appear in section 6.5 and Appendix E [M1]. Interpretation additionally requires knowing which components were available, how calibration was obtained, which future windows remained inside contiguous blocks and how much valid time remained. Future integration makes the full score retrospective. Positive product evidence and local correlation are different terms; a positive correlation alone does not demonstrate positive activation or a directional handoff. Contextual access ratings and autonomic outputs remain separately inspectable rather than silently changing the core measure.

CAA, Continuous Autonomic Arrays, restores when a change occurred. Beat intervals, heart-rate trends, rolling HRV, respiration and quality indicators can reveal an early response, sustained alteration or later recovery hidden by a branch mean. RR intervals are beat events, and rolling HRV represents a declared window rather than an instantaneous state. Breathing can account for part of an HRV change without making the breathing response irrelevant. Belt-derived depth is a relative measurement unless calibrated for another quantity. No single autonomic direction establishes narrative integration [M2].

## Micro Handoff and KHT: timing and reciprocal association

Micro Handoff asks whether a brief rise in temporal gamma is followed by a rise in slower theta/integration-like activity. It distinguishes state agreement, positive directional change and activation-weighted density. Jointly low signals can resemble one another; stationary high activity can contribute density without a handoff. Its fixed lag bank and baseline calibration constrain selection. The 25 ms output grid does not provide 25 ms spectral resolution: overlapping feature windows and smoothing affect the timing that can be interpreted.

KHT examines reciprocal statistical relationships between a gamma/work signal and a human-translation proxy; event-local variants additionally ask about later theta carryover. Local association and carryover are separate requirements in that proposal. Reciprocal regression coefficients do not establish reciprocal causation, and their product's sign does not establish a biological feedback mechanism. Shared inputs, common drivers, temporal dependence and physiology-selected anchors require explicit checks. A candidate discovered in one recording supplies a hypothesis to lock for a later recording [M3].

Synthetic recovery tests show whether a declared planted pattern can be recovered under specified conditions. They do not establish the human construct. The historical Micro Handoff record includes mixed directional contrasts and failed null criteria; that history remains relevant even when later software tests pass. Both families remain exploratory unless a particular endpoint is prospectively evaluated against independent outcomes. Their value is in making timing, persistence and alternative explanations testable while preserving uncertainty about the proposed mechanism.

## 6.5 CAI/SID and valid-time support

CAI is the **Controlled Access-Integration Index**; SID is **State Integration Density**. The frozen v0.2 core combines declared access-like, integration-like, positive-support and penalty features into a bounded unitless score [M1].

Read the normalization policy recorded for each result. The retained standalone core prefers Baseline 1 but permits declared full-run fallback and a standard-deviation scale when MAD is unusable. Its future interval is **8-30 seconds within the same continuous block**. The positive-amplitude product suppresses jointly below-midpoint contributions, while the worker's separate positive-correlation term can still contribute. This is a particular operational definition, not proof of integration; hard-invalid windows remain missing. Appendix E distinguishes these terms. Future support makes this an offline endpoint. Wholly missing NAS may be derived through NAST when coverage permits, with a separate enriched output and provenance. Partially observed NAS is not silently blended.

For accepted observations with nonoverlapping temporal support `dt_i`:

```text
T_valid = sum(dt_i over accepted observations)
CAI_load = sum(CAI_i * dt_i over accepted observations)
SID = CAI_load / T_valid
occupancy(tau) = sum(dt_i with CAI_i >= tau) / T_valid
```

SID is unitless, load is score-seconds and occupancy is a supported-time fraction. Zero valid time makes the ratios unavailable. Overlaps and duplicate timestamps must not multiply duration, and nominal support must not bridge long gaps. The valid-time layer reports its own uncertainty status.

Paired processing regenerates its canonical features using original calibration and records differences from older fallback policies. ## 6.6 Physiology and temporal interpretation

Sampled heart rate does not become observed RR intervals or HRV through reciprocation. RMSSD uses only valid pairs adjacent in the original beat ledger:

```text

```

Dropping an invalid interval before differencing can join nonadjacent beats. Gaps and connection boundaries therefore remain explicit; reviewed ectopic correction is a separate candidate. Raw interval variability is not automatically normal-to-normal HRV [M2].

PPG-derived PRV is distinct from ECG-derived HRV. Every trace should state its represented interval and processing support; a rolling estimate does not measure an instantaneous transition.

A **25 ms output grid is not 25 ms spectral resolution**. Different feature windows and smoothing contribute to fitted lags; scalp groups do not directly measure an STG-to-MTG transfer [M3].

KHT and related carryover analyses retain anchor and persistence rules. Reciprocal regressions do not establish reciprocal causation. Shared inputs, ordinary recovery and task relief require simpler alternatives and null-anchor comparisons. Synthetic lag recovery does not erase failed historical criteria.

# 8. Decoder Gate: design, predictions and results

## Research question

Decoder Gate asks whether prior context and assigned instructions change access to a story, comprehension, later recall and physiological recovery in distinguishable ways. Interpretive context has experimental precedents [R1], and divided attention is an established explanation for memory differences [R2]. A useful test must distinguish the proposed prediction from attention, effort, sensory access, arousal and ordinary memory processes.

The original D223/D224 design compared intact narrative, scrambled material and intact narrative under arithmetic load. Those branches do not complete a structure-by-instruction factorial design. The new behavioral pilot adds the fourth combination and uses different stories within a participant's session [S2, S4]. Decoder Gate and OptoPING remain separate proposals: a behavioral interaction would not identify an optical feedback mechanism.

## Two operational tests

The October 29 simulation tests whether context changes the physiological response to assigned surprise [S1]. For response `mu_j(c,s,tau)`, context `c` is binary, surprise `s` ranges from 0 to 1, `tau` is elapsed time after an event and `j` identifies the outcome. Holding nuisance inputs fixed:

```text
Delta_j(tau) = [mu_j(1,1,tau) - mu_j(1,0,tau)]
             - [mu_j(0,1,tau) - mu_j(0,0,tau)]
             = theta_j(tau)
```

This asks whether context changes the difference between high- and low-surprise responses. The model allows either sign and summarizes the contrast over 4-10 seconds. Those are assumed feature-response times, not measured neural transfer times. A flexible additive competitor controls nonlinear surprise effects; the interaction model adds context-by-surprise terms.

The October 30 extension tests signal structure and assigned stance using independently scored comprehension and delayed memory [S3]. Let `S=1` denote coherent material and `S=0` reordered material; let `R=1` denote narrative instruction and `R=0` arithmetic instruction. For correct-answer probability `p_sr`:

```text
Delta_behavior = (p_11 - p_01) - (p_10 - p_00)
```

This probability-scale contrast asks whether coherence has a different effect under the two instructions. With valid assignment and suitable participant/item analysis, it can estimate the total effect of those assignments. It is not interchangeable with a logistic-regression interaction coefficient.

A separate prediction test asks whether a fixed pre-probe physiological feature `P` improves prediction of answers beyond ordinary predictors, and whether its contribution varies by condition. The penalized logistic models are:

```text
B0: condition cells; flexible measured attention and arousal;
    ability, item difficulty, movement and session-state proxies.
B1: B0 + P + P^2 + P^3.
B2: B1 + P*S_c + P*R_c + P*S_c*R_c,
    where S_c = 2*S - 1 and R_c = 2*R - 1.

loss(y,p) = -[y*ln(p) + (1-y)*ln(1-p)]
gain = mean(loss_simpler - loss_extended)
```

B0-to-B1 gain measures generic physiological prediction; B1-to-B2 gain measures the additional condition-dependent relationship. Physiology must be specified without using the answer it predicts. Adjusting for attention or arousal measured after assignment tests conditional prediction, not automatically a direct causal gate effect: those variables may be mediators or colliders.

## Laptop feasibility pilot

Reading, Attention and Memory is an unnormed behavioral pilot, with managed integration in Public Workbench Alpha 11.10.0 [S4, S5]. It runs without EEG, LSL or XDF. Optional physiology is a separate measurement layer.

| --- | --- | --- |

The bank has 32 main stories, two practice items, four calibration items and eight balanced assignment lists. ## Decision rules and next use

The pilot first tests reading pace, answerability, scoring agreement, arithmetic burden, adherence and sensor reliability. Missing physiological support does not invalidate an otherwise usable behavioral answer. Failed arithmetic or inconvenient comprehension trials must not be removed to favor the hypothesis. Revised materials require a new version; exposed participants or items are not untouched evaluation data.

A later evaluation needs a prospectively chosen endpoint, meaningful effect scale, support rule and uncertainty method. Strong baselines matching or exceeding extended models challenge incremental prediction. For a prespecified meaningful effect `delta`, an appropriately constructed interval wholly within `[-delta, +delta]` supports a bounded negligible-effect conclusion; a wide interval is inconclusive. A directional prediction must also be frozen before evaluation. Missing condition coverage makes the comparison unavailable. Generalization requires fresh sessions, stimuli or participants.

Neither an interaction nor incremental prediction uniquely identifies a semantic gate. Observationally identical mechanisms require new discriminating measurements or interventions. Recognition, comprehension, recall, subjective impact and recovery remain separate outcomes. The simulations and software tests establish no human effect and authorize no live protocol adaptation.

# 9. OptoPING: physical feasibility and discriminating tests

The original OptoPING proposal is broader than photon-receptor feedback. It asks whether an intracellular structural mode could influence membrane excitability and participate in reciprocal coupling with electrical activity. The optional reconciliation appendix permits an optical, electrical, vibrational or mixed mode. Its proposed bridge is a cytosolic transfer process that converts that mode into a change in membrane threshold or forcing. Neither the carrier nor the transfer gain has been established by the equations [T10].

The meaning of `Y` therefore needs to remain explicit. D274 distinguishes an equation-level state, a simulated state, an inferred human transition state, a measured cellular proxy and a memory-relevant structural variable that survives competing explanations. These are different evidential claims. A fitted latent state in scalp EEG does not become a measured cellular mode, and a synthetic recovery does not identify the substrate [T11].

The early Rung 0 simulations test whether stipulated reciprocal couplings can be recovered from dynamical observations. They do not implement intracellular topology or the protocol's separate hypothesis that structural or regulatory refresh preserves a pattern through molecular turnover. Later feature and score tests address narrower discrimination questions under their declared generators; Appendix C.3 separates these stages [T10, L1].

The October 24 model takes a different step: it specifies a possible photon-sensitive receiver and asks whether its required local photon supply and response dynamics are physically plausible. Its receiver occupancy is not interchangeable with the early oscillatory `Y`. This is one candidate pathway within the broader proposal. Connecting the models requires measured source emission, transport, receiver sensitivity and coupling to electrical activity; their shared label does not supply that evidence [O6].

Persistent neural activity also has ordinary synaptic, circuit and metabolic explanations. Each proposed bridge must earn its place by predictions that those alternatives do not explain. The following photon-specific account retains the October 24 assumptions and results; it is not a microscopic QED derivation or a gamma-generating PING simulation [O6].

## The light supply required by a receiver

Let `y` be the fraction of receivers in an active state, `Phi` the local photon fluence rate within the effective activation band, `sigma` the absorption cross section and `q` the photoconversion probability. With activation rate `k` and deactivation rate `d = 1/tau_receiver`, the simple receiver model is:

```text
dy/dt = k (1 - y) - d y
k = q sigma Phi
y_equilibrium = k / (k + d)
Phi_required = y_equilibrium /
               [(1 - y_equilibrium) q sigma tau_receiver]
```

This connects a proposed active fraction to the light required to maintain it. It does not tell us which fraction would affect a neuron; that relationship needs independent measurement of the receiver and its downstream response.

The reference calculation assumes 100,000 receivers, `sigma = 2 x 10^-16 cm^2`, `q = 1` and a ten-second effective dark lifetime. None is established here for native cortical OPN5. The illustrative target is 1% occupancy, not a physiological detection threshold. Under these assumptions, with no reverse photoconversion:

| Local activation-band fluence, photons/cm^2/s | Unsaturated activation-count scale over 10 s | Equilibrium active fraction |
| ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `10^3` | `2 x 10^-7` | approximately `2 x 10^-12` |
| `10^6` | `0.0002` | approximately `2 x 10^-9` |
| `10^10` | `2` | approximately `0.000020` |
| `10^12` | `200` | approximately `0.001996` |
| `5.05 x 10^12` | `1,010` | `0.01` |

The count scale is `N q sigma Phi T`. It is not the number active at the endpoint: receivers deactivate and may activate again. Starting with inactive receivers, the reference fluence produces an exact expected active population of approximately 636 after ten seconds, rather than the equilibrium population of 1,000.

The distinction matters at low fluence. An expectation far below one activation is not a small activation delivered reliably to every cell. The simulation uses finite-state transition probabilities rather than turning fractional expected photon counts into guaranteed events. At high fluence, reverse photoconversion can also limit occupancy. An assumed stress case in Appendix C makes the 1% target unreachable; it is a test of the model's behavior, not measured OPN5 kinetics.

## What biological observations establish

Neural preparations emit ultraweak light. Isojima and colleagues measured approximately `10^-19 W/mm^2` from rat hippocampal slices; potassium depolarization increased emission and tetrodotoxin reduced it [O1]. Those observations support a relationship with neural activity and metabolism. They do not show that the escaping photons activated a receiving neuron.

Assigning all that external power to 380-nm photons would give approximately 19 photons/cm^2/s at the measurement surface. This is a conditional unit conversion, not a measured emission spectrum. It cannot be divided directly into the receiver budget above to obtain a defensible biological "photon deficit." Escaped light at a detector and illumination at a nearby receptor are different quantities. Collection geometry, absorption, escape fraction and source-receptor distance must connect them.

Kobayashi and colleagues observed brain emission associated with EEG activity, blood flow and oxidative conditions; glucose removal and mitochondrial inhibition changed it [O2]. This makes ordinary oxidative chemistry a serious explanation for changing light output. More emission need not mean more useful signaling. A proposed communication mechanism must explain what the photons do after production.

OPN5 provides a biologically grounded receiver candidate. Kojima and colleagues established near-380-nm sensitivity, Gi signaling and bistable photochemistry in reconstituted mammalian OPN5 [O3]. That preparation does not establish the abundance, absorption cross section, effective lifetime or feedback gain required by the cortical model. A persistent photoproduct is a candidate state variable, not a measured duration for the complete native pathway.

Zhang and colleagues demonstrated OPN5-dependent responses to external violet illumination in mouse hypothalamic neurons [O4]. Their attenuation measurements and sunlight extrapolation yielded a possible local violet flux of approximately `9 x 10^12 photons/cm^2/s`. This estimate concerns externally supplied light. It is neither an endogenous emission measurement nor a human cortical response threshold. Native response in that preparation supports investigating the candidate; it does not establish the proposed cortical source-receiver arrangement.

Tang and Dai's glutamate-evoked slice luminescence supplies another observation to examine [O5]. Their experiments included prolonged exposure to 50 mM glutamate and a gradual buildup over approximately 90 minutes. That preparation and time course do not establish physiological millisecond feedback or a naturally colocalized emitter-OPN5 pair.

Together, these studies justify investigating light production and reception. The missing link is quantitative: whether ordinary activity supplies enough photons in the appropriate band, at the appropriate location, to drive an independently measured receiver response and alter neural activity.

## What the simulations can distinguish

The physical-feasibility study compared optical, metabolic, synaptic, mixed and chemically confounded mechanisms under several perturbations [O6]. Under its assumed optical mechanism, strong photon-path suppression reduced the modeled activity and matched rescue restored it close to sham. The measured endpoint was activity during stimulation, not after-state persistence or EEG voltage. Simulation uncertainty describes repeated noise realizations at fixed parameters, not uncertainty about the biology.

The decisive result was the competing explanation. Optical and metabolic mechanisms could produce exactly the same training and sham trajectories. They separated under blockade only when the intervention was assumed to affect the optical pathway selectively. When a chemical alternative was allowed corresponding effects of both blocker and rescue, it reproduced the optical trajectories exactly. A convincing block-and-rescue pattern is therefore insufficient when the manipulations also change the competing chemical pathway.

Good prediction also failed to identify every parameter. Different activation-rate and feedback-gain combinations fitted the observations. The Rung 0 trajectory, feature and score exercises test different versions of recoverability under stipulated generators. Their favorable results are not biological sensitivity or specificity. Appendix C.3 now recovers that distinction and retains the null-test failures.

## Tests that could reject a specified pathway

A useful optical hypothesis must name its source, receiver, wavelengths, location and predicted effect size. The effective activation rate can then be bounded using measured spectral quantities:

```text
k_opt = integral[q(lambda) sigma(lambda) Phi(lambda) d(lambda)]
```

Here `Phi(lambda)` is local spectral photon fluence rate per unit wavelength, with matching wavelength units throughout the integral. Compare its supported upper bound with the minimum activation required by independently measured receiver kinetics and downstream gain. If even that upper supply bound cannot produce the proposed effect within those measured constraints, the specified model fails. An assumed gain or arbitrary 1% occupancy target cannot provide the independent minimum.

A second test concerns pathway dependence. A sufficiently strong, verified interruption of the proposed optical path should change the declared endpoint. If the endpoint remains unchanged within a prospectively specified informative interval, the experiment bounds or rejects that contribution for the preparation. Receiver-loss tests require consideration of compensation; optical blockade and rescue require chemical, electrical and thermal controls. A broad interval remains inconclusive.

Finally, compare predictions on perturbations not used to fit the model. If ordinary synaptic or metabolic alternatives predict those observations equally well, the available evidence does not identify an optical contribution. Near-field transfer, freely propagating photons and redox signaling require separate models. More measurements of a trajectory that all alternatives predict identically cannot resolve which mechanism produced it.

# Appendix A. Reconstructed mathematical specification

This appendix preserves the proposed encoding-storage-retrieval chain: activity changes a structural response, and a later cue preferentially reactivates the stored pattern. It separates that hypothesis from its possible membrane/network bridge and from human EEG measurements. The archived equations are specifications to investigate, not fitted descriptions of a demonstrated biological memory substrate [T1-T5].

**Status of the reconstruction.** The bounded phosphorylation equation and resonance hypothesis retain the archived architecture. The calcium monotonicity condition, oscillator peak, phase-locking statement and coupling conventions repair mathematical claims. The bounded refresh example and its stability calculation are explicitly **new formalizations**, offered to clarify the original turnover question. They are not recovered experimental findings or changes to Workbench analysis workers.

## A.1 Quantities, units and encoding

Time `t` is in seconds. Membrane voltage `V` and calcium reversal potential `E_Ca` are in volts; conductance `g` is in siemens; inward calcium current `I_Ca` is in amperes. Calcium concentration `C` and its baseline `C_0` use the same declared concentration unit. Phosphorylation fraction `P` lies between zero and one. Angular frequencies and decay rates have units `s^-1`, with radians treated as dimensionless.

The network patch makes encoding depend on integrated excitation, inhibition and plasticity gain [T2]. A dimensionally specified version is:

```text
V(t) = V_rest + integral_0^infinity h_m(r) I_net(t-r) dr
I_net = I_exc - I_inh + I_other
g_eff(t) = G_N(t) g_Ca_NMDA,              G_N(t) >= 0
I_Ca(t) = g_eff(t) s(t) B(V(t)) [E_Ca - V(t)]
```

Here `h_m` has units `V/(A s)`; `s` and magnesium-unblock factor `B` are dimensionless fractions. `g_Ca_NMDA` is a calibrated, nonnegative effective calcium-component conductance, not the total mixed-ion NMDA conductance. The influx expression is restricted to an inward-current regime, `V < E_Ca`. The calcium state equation below uses nonnegative influx. A model covering current reversal requires the corresponding ionic balances rather than silently using a negative concentration source.

`G_N` is an effective gain, potentially influenced by novelty, inhibition and neuromodulatory state. Calling it a novelty gate does not establish semantic specificity or imply that neuromodulators universally multiply NMDA conductance. Its physiological implementation and nonnegative parameter domain must be specified.

The archived claim that higher voltage necessarily increases influx needs an additional condition. Holding `g_eff` and `s` fixed:

```text
dI_Ca/dV = g_eff s [B'(V)(E_Ca - V) - B(V)]
dI_Ca/dV >= 0  iff  B'(V)(E_Ca - V) >= B(V)
```

The equivalence assumes `g_eff s > 0`. Increasing unblock competes with decreasing driving force. Monotonic `B` alone is insufficient; changing receptor opening or gain adds further terms. Familiar-versus-novel inequalities therefore need measured conditions or explicitly restricted assumptions.

A bounded encoding model is:

```text
dC/dt = -(C - C_0)/tau_C + alpha I_Ca
H(C) = C^n / (K_C^n + C^n)
dP/dt = k_ph H(C)(1-P) - k_deph P
```

Require `tau_C > 0`, `K_C > 0`, `n > 0`, `C_0 >= 0`, `alpha >= 0`, nonnegative rates and input, nonnegative initial concentration and `P(0)` in `[0,1]`. `alpha` has concentration units per coulomb; `k_ph` and `k_deph` are rates. Then concentration remains nonnegative. At `P=0`, its derivative is nonnegative; at `P=1`, it is nonpositive. Thus the continuous equation preserves `0 <= P <= 1`. A numerical solver must also respect this domain. Net `dP/dt` can be negative during dephosphorylation; it is not interchangeable with the positive encoding drive `k_ph H(C)(1-P)`.

## A.2 Stored tuning and selective retrieval

The original storage hypothesis is that biochemical state changes a measurable response function [T1,T2]:

```text
Omega_m(P) = Omega_0 + kappa_P P + O(P^2)
```

This is a local approximation, with `kappa_P` in `s^-1` per unit fraction and positive `Omega_m` over its intended domain. It does not establish whether the relevant mode is optical, electrical, mechanical or mixed. A mode-specific experiment must measure the change, its duration and its dependence on the proposed encoding process.

For a fixed stored state, consider the second-order driven oscillator:

```text
x'' + Gamma x' + Omega_m^2 x = F cos(Omega t)
A(Omega) = abs(F) / sqrt[(Omega_m^2-Omega^2)^2 + Gamma^2 Omega^2]
```

`x` has a declared response unit, `F` that unit per second squared, and `Gamma > 0`. The expression is the steady-state amplitude under constant forcing amplitude. A first-order complex optical-amplitude equation is a different representation; equivalence requires an explicit approximation and normalization.

For nonzero forcing, the exact amplitude peak is:

```text
Omega_peak = sqrt(Omega_m^2 - Gamma^2/2), if Gamma^2 < 2 Omega_m^2
Omega_peak = 0,                          otherwise
A(Omega_m) = abs(F)/(Gamma Omega_m)
```

Consequently, the peak is near the undamped natural frequency only with sufficiently weak damping. Near resonance, when `abs(Delta)` and `Gamma` are small relative to `Omega_m`:

```text
Delta = Omega - Omega_m
A(Delta) approximately abs(F)/(Omega_m sqrt(4 Delta^2 + Gamma^2))
A(Delta) > A_star requires:
abs(Delta) < 0.5 sqrt[(abs(F)/(A_star Omega_m))^2 - Gamma^2]
```

The bandwidth statement requires `A_star > 0`, a positive radicand and belongs to the approximation. If `F=0`, every frequency gives zero amplitude. A biological recall threshold `A_star` is an additional hypothesis, not a consequence of oscillator mechanics.

One scalar can represent only one tunable degree of freedom. The distributed extension instead uses a pattern of states and response functions across specified sites [T2]. A matching cue may activate enough of that pattern to distinguish it from alternatives. Demonstrating stronger response at a matched frequency would establish selective physical response; establishing memory additionally requires encoding history, cue specificity, behavioral relevance and controls for ordinary sensory tuning. The equations alone do not assign autobiographical content to a resonance.

## A.3 Maintenance under molecular turnover

The refresh proposal asks how an information-bearing organization could survive replacement of its material components [T3]. Its state vector is:

```text
X_mem = [P_tr, B, W, Z, R]
```

`P_tr` denotes structural trace strength, distinguished here from phosphorylation fraction `P`; their relationship must be specified. `B` denotes scaffold support, `W` circuit organization, `Z` regulatory support and `R` reactivation. These are distinct candidate variables, not five already measured components of a memory. Regulatory support does not mean a complete memory encoded as a DNA file.

The archived refresh law includes saturation, but unrestricted additive disturbances can drive a bounded trace negative. Its linear scaffold correction can also make loss negative. The following **new bounded realization** retains the proposed competition between maintenance and loss:

```text
p = P_tr/P_max,                          0 <= p <= 1
dp/dt = a(p,B,W,Z,R,t)(1-p) - b(B,t)p
a >= 0,                                 b >= 0
```

Both rates have units `s^-1`. For rates continuous in time and locally Lipschitz in the state, finite over each observation interval, the stated nonnegative boundary rates preserve the interval. One possible scaffold-dependent loss is `b=lambda_0/(1+rho B)` for nonnegative, normalized `B` and `rho`; this illustrative choice is not an identified biological law. Fluctuations require a process that preserves the domain, rather than arbitrary additive noise followed by unreported clipping.

A scalar example isolates what a refresh threshold can and cannot mean:

```text
dp/dt = (a_ext + k_s p)(1-p) - lambda p
a_ext >= 0, k_s >= 0, lambda > 0
```

With `a_ext=0`, a positive equilibrium `p*=1-lambda/k_s` exists when `k_s>lambda`, and is locally stable. For `k_s<lambda`, zero is stable and positive traces decay. At equality, decay is algebraic rather than exponential. Exact zero remains zero without a seed even when the positive equilibrium exists.

That threshold is specific to self-dependent renewal in this example. With `k_s=0` and sustained external support, `p*=a_ext/(a_ext+lambda)>0` for every positive `a_ext`, including `a_ext/lambda<1`. Therefore the archived aggregate ratio cannot universally imply that every subunit ratio entails complete trace loss. A positive driven level also does not establish a self-sustaining memory.

For the full coupled system, a proposed equilibrium must satisfy all state equations. At an equilibrium of a continuously differentiable autonomous system, a Jacobian with eigenvalues having negative real parts gives local asymptotic stability; it does not prove global attraction or preservation of information. Instantaneous eigenvalues are not sufficient for a time-varying system. Trace identity requires a separately defined pattern-distance or retrieval criterion. A scalar amount can persist while its encoded pattern changes. The public refresh note's shorthand adds copy error and noise before dividing by a correction gain [T12]. That expression does not specify whether noise is a drift, amplitude or variance. It cannot supply a general fidelity threshold without a stochastic model.

**New formalization for local fidelity error.** Let e_f be one signed, calibrated error coordinate relative to a specified target pattern. It has a declared unit U. For a fixed target and constant coefficients, one illustrative correction process is:

```text
de_f = (b_f - k_f e_f) dt + sigma_f dW_t,       k_f > 0
E[e_f(t)] = e_f(0) exp(-k_f t)
           + (b_f/k_f)[1-exp(-k_f t)]
Var[e_f(t)] = sigma_f^2[1-exp(-2 k_f t)]/(2 k_f)
E_stationary[e_f] = b_f/k_f
Var_stationary[e_f] = sigma_f^2/(2 k_f)
MSE_stationary = (b_f/k_f)^2 + sigma_f^2/(2 k_f)
```

The finite-time variance assumes a deterministic initial error, with sigma_f >= 0 by convention. W_t is a standard Wiener process. k_f has units s^-1, b_f has units U/s, and sigma_f has units U/sqrt(s). Both terms in the mean squared error have units U^2. A nonzero mean error from systematic drift is distinct from variance caused by stochastic disturbances. The stationary identities follow by setting the mean equation m'=b_f-k_f m and variance equation v'=-2 k_f v+sigma_f^2 to zero.

For sigma_f>0, the stationary distribution is Gaussian with mean mu=b_f/k_f and standard deviation s_f=sigma_f/sqrt(2 k_f). At a stationary observation time, for a fixed tolerance d_f>0:

```text
Pr(abs(e_f) <= d_f)
  = F_N[(d_f-mu)/s_f] - F_N[(-d_f-mu)/s_f]
```

F_N is the standard normal cumulative distribution function. When sigma_f=0, the stationary error is deterministic and should be evaluated directly. A study must select its tolerance, required probability or MSE limit, retention duration and readout independently of the result. None is numerically calibrated here.

This Ornstein-Uhlenbeck example is a local model of signed error, not a bounded structural fraction or a nonnegative distance. Its Gaussian support is unbounded. A high pointwise retention probability does not guarantee that the error remains inside tolerance throughout a long interval. Continuous retention requires a first-exit or finite-horizon analysis. Multidimensional pattern identity needs an explicit error geometry and covariance model; one coordinate cannot certify an arbitrary memory.

Changing targets, correlated disturbances, nonlinear correction and shifting cellular representations may invalidate the example. A trace readout should allow a declared form of representational change while retaining independently measured content. Neither these equations nor a good fit establish that epigenetic processes perform the modeled correction. They supply a dimensional and probabilistic replacement for the earlier shorthand, to be compared with ordinary maintenance models.

## A.4 Structural coupling and phase effects

The candidate bridge adds a structural contribution to membrane or network dynamics [T4]:

```text
Y(t) = integral_0^infinity h_cyto(r) M_OS(t-r) dr
theta_E(t) = theta_E0 - eta_E Y(t)
```

The kernel must carry the units converting the measured mode into `Y`; `eta_E` converts `Y` into the chosen threshold unit. These coefficients must be estimated. The notation does not turn an unspecified intracellular process into a measured causal pathway.

An optical carrier cannot itself be EEG gamma. An intensity envelope, beat or electrogenic transduction would require a physical route with adequate amplitude and timing. A sustained difference-frequency signal additionally requires suitable coherence and detection/transduction; writing two frequencies does not establish those conditions. Low-frequency structural or electrical alternatives require their own measurements and are not excluded by a failed freely propagating photon budget.

For a specified slow phase difference, a stochastic Adler model is:

```text
dpsi = [Delta_omega - K sin(psi)] dt + sqrt(2D) dW_t
```

`K >= 0` and `Delta_omega` are rates, `D` is phase-diffusion strength in radians squared per second, and `W_t` is a Wiener process. In the noiseless model a stable fixed phase exists for `abs(Delta_omega)<K`. Equality is a marginal boundary. With noise, the archived rule `K > abs(Delta_omega)+D` is not a universal locking threshold. Phase slips and finite-window phase concentration depend on the parameters and observation period. Comparisons need matched common-drive and surrogate controls; increased phase-locking value alone does not identify structural coupling.

## A.5 Reciprocal coupling, stability and identifiability

A local continuous-time approximation to two coupled states is:

```text
de/dt = -a_e e + eta y + B_u u
dy/dt = -a_y y + zeta e + C_u u
K_loop = eta zeta
K_mag = sqrt(abs(K_loop))
```

For state units `U_e` and `U_y`, `eta` has units `U_e/(U_y s)` and `zeta` has units `U_y/(U_e s)`. Their product has units `s^-2`; `K_mag` has units `s^-1`. These units apply only to this two-first-order-state approximation. The original Rung 0 model couples into `dE/dt` and `d^2Y/dt^2`, so its product instead has units `s^-3` under physical time. Models with different orders or normalizations require separate coupling definitions; their numerical K values are not interchangeable. Report the sign of `K_loop` separately. Taking an absolute value must not erase the distinction between reinforcing and opposing feedback.

Under a constant nonzero rescaling `y_new=c y`, the coefficients become `eta/c` and `c zeta`; their product is unchanged. This explains one reason directional coefficients may not be separately recoverable without calibration. It does not guarantee that even the product is identifiable from a particular recording, sampling scheme or model.

For this two-state, instantaneous system with positive decay rates, local stability requires:

```text
a_e a_y - eta zeta > 0
```

The trace is already negative. A sufficiently large positive loop destabilizes this linearization; a coupling magnitude is not automatically a measure of stable persistence. Delays, additional states and nonlinear saturation require their own analysis.

For the discrete model in the human translation [T5], stability instead requires all transition-matrix eigenvalues inside the unit circle. Discrete coefficients depend on sampling interval and should not be equated numerically with continuous-time rates. Shared inputs, correlated errors, observation noise and constructing `y` partly from `e` can generate apparent reciprocal prediction without a separate causal state. Source identity, independent observations and interventions matter as much as fitted coefficients.

## A.6 What persistence would establish

A transient can decay slowly without being an attractor. Recurrence means returning near an earlier observed state; common input can produce it. An attractor is a property of specified dynamics and a basin of initial conditions, not a label earned by repeated EEG features. Persistence during continued stimulation differs from persistence after the drive ends.

Topo-OSM can operationalize human state geometry [T5], provided variables, distances, graph construction and controls are declared. Such geometry is not automatically the intracellular structure proposed by OSM. The reconstruction keeps both questions available without treating one as evidence for the other.

Independent numerical checks accompany this reconstruction for bounds, response peaks, illustrative refresh equilibria and coupling rescaling. Finite checks can expose implementation errors; the derivations establish the stated conditional results. Neither supplies fitted biological values, validates a memory substrate or demonstrates a new human effect.

## A.7 Updating alignment and predictive validity

These **candidate operational definitions** refine D198 [T6]. They do not define all meaning or establish an empathy endpoint. They are mathematical specifications for prospective testing, with no change to historical Workbench scores.

### A.7.1 Distributional updating

For participant i and experience u, define probability distributions q_pre and q_post over the same declared variable z. With densities relative to the same measure, use:

```text
U_i(u) = D_KL(q_post || q_pre)
       = integral q_post(z) ln[q_post(z)/q_pre(z)] dz
```

Use a sum for a discrete state space. U_i is nonnegative and equals zero exactly when the distributions agree almost everywhere. Natural logarithms give nats; base-two logarithms give bits. KL is asymmetric and is not a distance metric. Finite divergence requires absolute continuity of q_post with respect to q_pre and finite integral. Positive posterior probability on a set assigned zero prior probability produces infinite divergence rather than a silently repaired finite value.

Collect prior judgments before exposure and posterior judgments afterward about the same propositions or outcomes. Specify normalization, probability elicitation, missingness and any probability floor before collection. A floor changes the reported distributions and must be disclosed. If a fitted latent-state model supplies these probabilities, freeze its likelihood, fitting procedure and calibration on separate development data. Two elicited distributions need not satisfy Bayes' rule; call their divergence reported-belief updating unless the Bayesian model is actually specified and tested. Post-only ratings cannot recover an unmeasured prior.

The equation is closely related to established Bayesian-surprise formulations [R9]. It is not a claim of mathematical priority. Large U_i can accompany inaccurate updating; small U_i can coexist with recognition and strong personal relevance. Estimation noise can also create positive divergence when true change is absent. Quantify reliability and uncertainty before interpreting a small value, and avoid treating fitted latent probabilities as direct observations of neural states.

### A.7.2 Alignment in common coordinates

Use a declared map F_i to place each person's measured coordinates in a common space. Then define a change vector and metric cosine:

```text
m_i = F_i(eta_i,post) - F_i(eta_i,pre)
A_ij = (m_i^T G m_j) /
       sqrt[(m_i^T G m_i)(m_j^T G m_j)]
```

G is a fixed symmetric positive-definite matrix, and both vectors must be nonzero. The Cauchy-Schwarz inequality in the G inner product gives -1 <= A_ij <= 1. The metric must account for coordinate scales and units. Report the two update magnitudes separately; equal direction does not imply equal change. Zero-vector alignment is undefined. Near-zero or poorly measured vectors require a prespecified reliability rule and uncertainty analysis, not an arbitrary denominator constant that manufactures a value.

Freeze mappings, metric and feature selection independently of the evaluation outcomes. Independently fitted participant embeddings are not automatically comparable. Under an invertible linear coordinate change m'=A m, preserve the score with G'=A^(-T) G A^(-1). Arbitrary nonlinear transformations do not preserve finite differences. This cosine is also not automatically the information geometry of the KL divergence; a common notation does not establish that connection.

### A.7.3 Predictive validity on independent outcomes

D198's truth-tracking intention is retained as a narrower, testable improvement in prediction. Let p_pre,j and p_post,j be forecasts for the same externally assessed binary outcome y_j. Both forecasts must be fixed before y_j is revealed and before any scoring feedback. For n accepted, identically paired outcomes:

```text
L_Brier(p,y) = (p-y)^2,                  0 <= p <= 1, y in {0,1}
Delta V_hat = (1/n) sum_j [L_Brier(p_pre,j,y_j)
                         - L_Brier(p_post,j,y_j)]
```

Positive Delta V_hat indicates lower observed prediction loss after exposure on this sample. This is an operational predictive-validity score, not a general measure of truth. For a Bernoulli outcome with true probability r, the expected Brier loss is (p-r)^2+r(1-r), uniquely minimized at p=r. That is the proper-scoring justification [R11]. A preregistered logarithmic loss is another option, with its zero-probability behavior made explicit. Do not select a score after seeing which one favors the hypothesis.

Pre and post forecasts must face the same outcome distribution and the same scoring key. If latent beliefs q are translated into forecasts p, declare and freeze that predictive mapping too. Test items, later facts or other outcomes used for evaluation must be unavailable to fitting, feature selection and participant feedback at the time of prediction. Probability change by itself says nothing about whether the error improves. For example, moving from p=0.5 to p=0.9 reduces Brier loss by 0.24 if y=1, but increases it by 0.56 if y=0. These are arithmetic examples, not experimental observations.

Report paired uncertainty at the participant and item level appropriate to the design, missing and excluded outcomes, calibration and any measurement error in the outcome. Overlapping windows and repeatedly scored answers are not new independent observations. Precision too low to exclude a meaningful improvement leaves the claim unresolved. Recall of a fictional detail supports story accuracy only; a claim about real-world validity needs independently verifiable real-world outcomes.

## A.8 A dimensionally consistent meaning-arc candidate

D205's original `M_arc = Var[SMD] + max|dSMD/dt|` adds a dimensionless variance to an inverse-time quantity. A repaired candidate declares a bounded dimensionless coding trace `S(t)`, a fixed observation interval `[0,T]` and a positive reference duration `T0` [T8]:

```text
S_mean = (1/T) integral_0^T S(t) dt
S_dose = integral_0^T S(t) dt
V_S = (1/T) integral_0^T [S(t) - S_mean]^2 dt
```

`S_mean` is a mean, while `S_dose` has units of time and accumulates coded exposure; they must not share a label that implies the same quantity. Both arc components are dimensionless. Freeze `T0`, nonnegative weights `a,b`, coding rules, sampling grid, smoothing and derivative estimator before evaluation. Their values are not fitted here. A discontinuous idealized trace may have no finite classical derivative; the specified discrete estimator is then a different measured quantity. Missing intervals require explicit support handling rather than invented continuity.

# Appendix C. Optical model and reproducibility testbeds

## C.1 Reduced dynamics and photon transport

The October 24 model uses dimensionless excitatory and inhibitory activity envelopes `E` and `I`, metabolic state `z`, active receiver fraction `y` and downstream state `s` [O6]. `E` is not an electric-field measurement. Phi_received = Phi_base (g + r)

```text
dy/dt = k_on (1 - y) - d y
ds/dt = (y - s) / tau_s
```
